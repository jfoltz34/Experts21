# Construction Planning & Critical-Path Scheduling

## Benchmark durations (all **Estimates**)

| Phase | Second-generation space | Cold shell |
|---|---|---|
| Site search → signed lease (with contingencies) | 1-3 mo | 1-3 mo |
| Design + engineering + DBPR plan review | 2-3 mo | 3-4 mo |
| City of Tampa Alcoholic Beverage Special Use (S-2) | 3-6 mo (in parallel) | 3-6 mo |
| Building permit | 1-3 mo | 2-4 mo |
| Construction | 8-14 wks | 5-8 mo |
| Inspections (plumbing, hood/Ansul, fire, health, building final) | 2-4 wks | 2-4 wks |
| Hiring + training (managers start 3-4 mo before opening; hourly staff 2-3 wks of training) | overlaps | overlaps |
| Soft opening → grand opening | 1-2 wks | 1-2 wks |
| **Typical lease signing → opening** | **6-9 mo** | **9-14 mo** |

## Procedure

1. Build the schedule backward from the target opening date. Avoid opening between June and September, the slow season and hurricane season. Aim for **October-January** so the restaurant ramps into the January-April peak.
2. Identify the critical path. It is usually the alcohol special use and the building permit, then construction, then inspections.
3. Run long-lead procurement early: hood, walk-ins, custom millwork, patio structures and fans, and lighting (8-16 weeks).
4. Tie rent commencement to the certificate of occupancy, and tie the GMP contract to the schedule with delay provisions.
5. Each week, track planned vs. actual, critical-path float, and the cost of delay. Each month of delay costs roughly rent + management payroll + interest + lost margin; quantify it with fpa-analyst.
6. Hurricane season (June 1-November 30): build weather float into the schedule and secure the site during storms.

## Output

A milestone table (milestone, owner, start, finish, dependency, status), the critical path, risks to the date, and the cost of each month of slip.
