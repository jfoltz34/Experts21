# Facilities & Energy Management

## Florida realities

- High heat and humidity most of the year, so air conditioning is a top-three controllable cost. Utilities run 3-4% of sales in the model.
- Patio comfort decides whether patio seats sell from May through October: shade, ceiling fans, misters or evaporative coolers, and rain cover.
- Hurricane season (June-November) brings power outages, so refrigeration and generator planning matter (see `hurricane-continuity`).

## Design guidance

- Size HVAC for the kitchen heat load plus full occupancy plus make-up air from the hood. Use dedicated dehumidification to prevent condensation and mold.
- Prefer high-efficiency rooftop units (high SEER2), demand-controlled kitchen ventilation, LED lighting with dimming scenes, and ENERGY STAR refrigeration and dish machines.
- Provide a generator transfer switch or quick-connect for the walk-ins and critical circuits.
- Install water filtration for espresso and ice. Hard Florida water scales equipment.

## Preventive maintenance calendar

| Frequency | Tasks |
|---|---|
| Daily | Temperature logs for coolers and freezers, hood filter check, grease trap check |
| Weekly | Ice machine sanitizing (per manufacturer), espresso backflush |
| Monthly | HVAC filter change, condenser coil check, patio fan and mister service |
| Quarterly | Hood cleaning (frequency per volume and code), refrigeration service |
| Semi-annual | Fire suppression (Ansul) inspection, HVAC service before summer and before winter |
| Annual | Backflow test, fire extinguisher certification, roof inspection before hurricane season |

## Procedure

1. Estimate utility costs by month, with summer peaks. Benchmark against 3-4% of sales.
2. Calculate the payback on efficiency upgrades (cost ÷ annual savings). Recommend if payback is under 3-4 years.
3. Keep a service-vendor list with emergency contacts and response-time terms.

## Output

Specs or recommendations, cost and payback estimates, and the maintenance calendar.
