# Hospitality & Service Design

## Guest journey (map every touchpoint)

Discover (social, Google, concierge), book (Resy/OpenTable, deposit for 6+ guests), arrive (parking or valet, host greeting by name), seat, first touch, order, food delivery, check-backs, dessert or pastry offer, check, farewell, follow-up (review request, rebooking).

## Steps of service (upscale brunch, Estimate timings)

1. Greet within 1 minute of seating. Water, plus coffee offered **immediately**: brunch guests want caffeine first.
2. Drinks order within 3 minutes. A welcome bite or mini-pastry is a signature hospitality touch that protects perceived value.
3. Food order within 8-10 minutes. The server suggests one shareable "moment" item for the table.
4. Food arrives in 15-18 minutes (weekend target). Check back within 2 bites.
5. Coffee refills and table maintenance without being asked.
6. Offer pastry to go or a retail item with the check, to build weekday habit.
7. Target table time at peak: 75-90 minutes for 2-4 guests, 105-120 minutes for 6+. Plan turns accordingly.

## Reservations and waitlist

- Hold about 70-80% of weekend capacity for reservations and the rest for walk-ins, managed by a waitlist with SMS.
- Require card holds or deposits for parties of 6+ and holidays. Send confirmation and reminder messages.
- Pace seatings to protect the kitchen. Cap covers per 15-minute slot.

## SOPs to write

Opening and closing checklists, side work, table numbering and positions, allergy protocol (flag in POS, verbal confirm, manager check), large-party service, the private-room event flow, and the alcohol-service policy (ID checks, over-service).

## Service recovery playbook (LAST)

**L**isten, **A**pologize, **S**olve (comp an item or dessert, or rebook), **T**hank. The manager visits every table at weekend peak. Respond to reviews within 48 hours. Log the incident and the fix.

## Output

A journey map, step-of-service standards with timings, SOP outlines, and the KPIs: ticket times, table time, rating, complaint rate.
