# Startup Budgeting & Cost Estimation

## Baseline budget (from docs/04-startup-costs.md)

For about 4,000 sq ft of second-generation space (all figures **Estimates**):

| Line | Low | Base | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deposits + pre-opening rent | \$40K | \$75K | \$120K |
| Design / architecture / engineering | \$60K | \$100K | \$160K |
| Construction (\$150-300/sf, second-generation) | \$600K | \$850K | \$1.2M |
| Bar build | \$60K | \$100K | \$150K |
| Kitchen equipment | \$120K | \$180K | \$260K |
| FF&E incl. patio cooling | \$200K | \$325K | \$500K |
| Technology | \$30K | \$50K | \$75K |
| Smallwares | \$35K | \$55K | \$80K |
| Liquor license (SRX path) | \$2K | \$2.5K | \$2.5K |
| Other permits / legal / accounting | \$25K | \$45K | \$80K |
| Opening inventory | \$30K | \$45K | \$65K |
| Pre-opening payroll and training | \$90K | \$140K | \$200K |
| Pre-opening marketing | \$40K | \$70K | \$110K |
| Insurance / utility deposits | \$20K | \$35K | \$60K |
| Working-capital reserve | \$200K | \$300K | \$450K |
| Contingency (~15%) | \$210K | \$290K | \$440K |
| **Total** | **≈\$1.76M** | **≈\$2.66M** | **≈\$3.95M** |

Buying a quota 4COP liquor license instead of the SRX path adds about \$210-250K.

## Benchmarks

- Second-generation vs. cold shell: 30-50% cheaper.
- Casual full-service build-out: \$220-300/sf in second-generation space, \$280-360/sf in cold shell. Upscale or bar-forward: \$320-440+/sf.
- A bar program adds \$40-80/sf.
- Owner-supplied FF&E for an upscale concept: \$300-800K+.
- Grease trap: \$3-8K interior; exterior interceptor \$15-40K.
- First-time operators overrun by 20-35%.

## Procedure

1. Tie every line to a source: a quote, a bid, or a benchmark with the quantity used.
2. Net the landlord tenant-improvement allowance (TIA) against construction. Show the budget both gross and net.
3. Keep contingency at 15% or more until the guaranteed-maximum-price (GMP) contract is signed. Never use contingency to fund scope creep without recording it.
4. Track budget vs. committed vs. spent each week during construction.
5. Look for savings, in this order:
   - Take over a closed restaurant's space with its equipment.
   - Negotiate a bigger TIA.
   - Lease equipment.
   - Buy certified pre-owned equipment.
   - Value-engineer finishes.
   - Never cut the reserve.
6. Remember that dropping below 2,500 sq ft of service area or 150 seats loses SRX eligibility.

## Output

A budget table (gross, TIA, net), the variance vs. baseline with reasons, and the top three cost risks.
