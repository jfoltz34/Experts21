# 04 — Startup Costs

_Research compiled November 2026. Published benchmarks are cited. The Tampa-specific budget is an **Estimate** built from those benchmarks. Get contractor bids, equipment quotes and a broker opinion before relying on it._

## Reference concept (assumption for all cost modeling)

| Parameter | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Format | Upscale, full-service, daytime-focused brunch (about 8am–3pm daily), full bar |
| Size | ~4,000 sq ft interior + ~800–1,200 sq ft covered patio |
| Seats | ~130 interior + ~30–40 patio (needed to qualify for the SRX liquor license; see [06-permits-and-licenses.md](06-permits-and-licenses.md)) |
| Location | South Tampa / Hyde Park / SoHo, Water Street / Channel District, or Midtown |
| Space type | **Second-generation** restaurant space (existing hood, grease interceptor, MEP) strongly preferred |

**Ways to bring the total down:**
- **Take over a closed restaurant's space with its equipment** (several 2026 closures in Hyde Park/SoHo). This can cut construction and equipment by 30–50% and reach a total of roughly **$1.2M–$1.8M**.
- **Negotiate a landlord TIA** of $50–$150/sf, common for strong operators in new developments like Water Street and Midtown.
- **Lease kitchen equipment** and buy certified pre-owned items.
- **Shrink to about 3,000 sq ft**. Note that going below 2,500 sq ft of service area or 150 seats makes the full-liquor SRX license unavailable, which forces a beer/wine-only license or a quota license purchase.

## Capital structure considerations

- **SBA 7(a)** loans (up to $5M) are the most common debt for independent restaurants. Expect a 10–30% equity injection and a personal guarantee.
- **Equipment financing/leasing** keeps cash free for working capital.
- **Investor equity** is typical for upscale concepts. Model investor returns over a 2–3 year break-even horizon (industry norm).
- **Key ratio:** sales-to-investment of at least 1.5:1 in year 2 (e.g., a $2.5M+ investment needs about $3.75M sales). This is aggressive for a daytime-only concept, which is why controlling build-out cost matters so much. See [05-operating-costs.md](05-operating-costs.md).
