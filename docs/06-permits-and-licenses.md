# 06 — Permits and Licenses (Tampa / Hillsborough County / Florida)

_Research compiled November 2026. This is a planning checklist, **not legal advice**. Fees and rules change, so confirm each item with the issuing agency and engage a Florida alcoholic-beverage attorney and a local permit expediter. Items marked **Verify** have not been confirmed against a primary source in this research._

## Critical-path summary

The **alcohol license and zoning approval** is the longest-lead, highest-stakes item. It shapes the real estate decision: square footage, seat count, distance from schools and churches, and the zoning district. **Do not sign a lease without an alcohol contingency.**

## 2. Food service license (state)

**Florida DBPR, Division of Hotels & Restaurants (DHR).** In Florida, restaurant food licensing is done by the state (DBPR), not the county health department.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Plan review | Required before construction for new or remodeled food establishments. **Fee: $0** (DBPR) |
| Application fee | **$50** (new license or change of ownership) |
| Permanent Food Service, seating, **150–249 seats** | **$294/yr** (full-year) or $152 (half-year), includes $10 Hospitality Education Program fee |
| 50–149 seats | $273/yr |
| Renewal | Hillsborough is DBPR **District 3**; licenses renew annually on **Feb 1** |
| Opening inspection | DHR inspector must pass the establishment before opening |
| **Certified Food Protection Manager** | Florida requires certified food managers (FS 509.039). Plan on certifying the GM, chef, sous chef and all shift managers |
| **Employee food handler training** | Required for all food employees within 60 days of hire (FS 509.049) via a DBPR-approved provider |

## 3. Alcoholic beverage license (state) — the key decision

**Florida DBPR, Division of Alcoholic Beverages & Tobacco (ABT).**

| Option | What it allows | Requirements | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **2COP** | Beer + wine, on-premises | No quota; simpler | Low annual fee (a few hundred dollars by county tier). **Verify** exact Hillsborough fee |
| **4COP-SRX (Special Restaurant License)** — *recommended path* | Beer, wine **and liquor** (on-premises only) | Licensed premises of **≥2,500 sq ft of service area**, equipped to serve meals to **≥150 guests at one time** (150 seats), and **≥51% of gross food & beverage revenue from food and non-alcoholic beverages**. Ongoing revenue audits. Cannot be sold or transferred like a quota license | ≈**$1,820/yr** in large counties like Hillsborough |
| **4COP Quota** | Beer, wine, liquor; no food requirement; transferable asset | Must buy an existing quota license on the secondary market (or win a lottery for new ones) | Annual ≈$1,820 **plus** a purchase price of **≈$210K–$250K in Hillsborough** (recent listings: $210K, $240K) |

**Recommendation:** Design the space for **≥2,500 sq ft of service area and ≥150 seats** to qualify for SRX. Brunch's food-heavy sales mix should meet the 51% food test comfortably, but **bottomless-drink programs push the alcohol share up**, so track the ratio monthly. Alcohol counsel should also confirm **whether patio seats count toward the 150** for the specific site plan (**Verify**).

**Bottomless mimosas:** These are common at Tampa brunches, but confirm current ABT rules and the liability insurance implications with counsel before marketing an "unlimited" program (**Verify**).

## 4. City of Tampa zoning: alcoholic beverage sales

- On-premises alcohol sales in the City of Tampa generally require **Alcoholic Beverage (AB) Sales Special Use approval**: an administrative **Special Use 1 (S-1)** or a public-hearing **Special Use 2 (S-2 / "AB2")** that goes before City Council, depending on the zoning district, the license type, and whether the restaurant meets the code's restaurant criteria (Tampa Code Chapter 27).
- An S-2 application needs: a full description of the proposed use, a **24"×36" AB site plan**, a floor plan with occupancy, a survey with an **"AB Sketch"** showing distance separations, and waiver or variance requests where distance rules aren't met.
- **Distance separation rules** apply relative to schools, churches/places of worship, residential uses, and other AB establishments. Waivers are possible but add hearing risk.
- **Timeline (Estimate):** 3–6 months for S-2, including City Council hearings. Some sites in districts already approved for restaurant AB sales can move faster. **Check whether the target site has existing AB approval it can transfer to the new operator.** Second-generation restaurant sites often do, which is a major schedule advantage.
- **Hours of sale:** Confirm City of Tampa hours for alcohol sales, especially **Sunday morning start times**, which are critical for brunch (**Verify** in Tampa Code Ch. 3).

## Suggested sequencing

1. Engage alcohol attorney + permit expediter → screen sites for **zoning/AB approval status**, distance rules, flood zone, and historic district.
2. LOI → lease with contingencies (AB approval, permits, TIA, delivery condition).
3. Concurrently: entity, EIN, DOR registration, DBPR plan review, ABT application, S-1/S-2 application.
4. Building and fire permits → construction → inspections.
5. DBPR opening inspection, ABT final inspection / license issuance, CO, business tax receipts.
6. Food manager certification and staff food-handler training before soft opening.
