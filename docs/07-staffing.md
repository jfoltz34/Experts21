# 07 — Staffing

_Research compiled November 2026. Wage data comes from the U.S. BLS (Tampa–St. Petersburg–Clearwater MSA) and Florida minimum-wage law. Headcounts and pay plans are **Estimates** for the base-case concept (about $3.5M sales, about 160 seats, 8am–3pm daily)._

## Labor market context

- **Florida minimum wage rose to $15.00/hr on Sept 30, 2026**, and the **tipped cash wage to $11.98/hr** (max tip credit $3.02). Starting Sept 30, 2027 it is indexed to inflation annually.
- Tipped employees must average **more than $30/month in tips** to count as tipped under Florida's definition (DOL table).
- **BLS May 2023, Tampa MSA** medians (pre-dating the latest increases, so real rates are now higher):

- Food prep and serving is about **8.8% of Tampa-area employment**, a big labor pool (BLS May 2024), but there is **heavy competition for skilled cooks** from the MICHELIN-era dining scene, hotels and theme-park employers in the broader region.
- **Daytime-only hours are a recruiting advantage**: no late nights, and evenings free for family or school (USF, UT and HCC students).

**Total:** about **55–70 employees** (mostly part-time), about **35–45 FTE**. Weekend shifts need roughly twice the weekday staffing.

## Compliance essentials

- **Tip pooling:** Under the FLSA, managers and supervisors may not keep tips. Back-of-house can share in a tip pool **only if no tip credit is taken** for any participant. Decide the model (traditional tip credit vs. full minimum wage + service charge + BOH pool) with labor counsel.
- **Service charges** (e.g., automatic 20% for parties of 8+) are restaurant revenue, not tips. They are taxed and distributed differently, so disclose them clearly.
- **E-Verify** is mandatory for Florida employers with 25+ employees.
- **Food safety:** certified food protection managers on every shift; food-handler training for all staff within 60 days.
- **Alcohol:** responsible vendor training for all alcohol servers; checking ID; dram-shop awareness.
- **Minors:** follow Florida child labor rules for 16–17-year-old hosts and bussers (hour limits, alcohol-service restrictions).
- **Overtime:** Weekend-heavy schedules can push full-timers past 40 hours. Use scheduling software to cap them.

## Recruiting and retention strategy

1. **Hire leadership 3–4 months before opening.** The GM and Executive Chef shape the concept, menu and hiring.
2. **Hiring sources:** Culinary Poptential and Hillsborough Community College culinary programs, Indeed/Culinary Agents/Poached, poaching from top-rated competitors, hospitality groups, and job fairs. Openings after the 2026 closures (e.g., Rome + Fig) may free up experienced staff.
3. **Pre-opening training:** 2–3 weeks, including menu tastings, wine and cocktail training, steps of service, POS, and mock services (friends and family).
4. **Retention levers:** daytime schedule, predictable weekends-on/weekdays-off rotations, a clear career ladder (runner → server → captain → manager; prep → line → sous), shift meals, a quarterly bonus pool tied to reviews and sales, and health benefits for full-time staff.
5. **Turnover benchmark:** Restaurant hourly turnover often runs 70%+ annually. Target under 50% for a culture-led independent restaurant.

## Labor cost targets

- **Total labor (incl. salaried, taxes, benefits): 32–36% of sales.**
- **Hourly labor by daypart:** track labor $ per cover (target about $9–$12) and sales per labor hour (SPLH target ≥$55–$70).
- Use **demand-based scheduling** from reservation data (Resy/OpenTable feeds) to staff the weekend peaks without overstaffing weekdays.
