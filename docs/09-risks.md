# 09 — Biggest Risks and Mitigations

_Research compiled November 2026. Risks are ranked by **likelihood × impact** for the reference concept (upscale, daytime-focused brunch, about 160 seats, Tampa). Ratings are analyst judgments._

### 1. Weak weekday demand / breakfast decline 🔴
**Evidence:** Ciccio Restaurant Group closed **Daily Eats** (a 20-year SoHo brunch staple) in June 2026 and is reopening it as Meeting House, focused on lunch and dinner. They said weekday breakfast was "too slow" and "the one meal people are skipping right now is breakfast," and cited GLP-1 adoption. Mintel describes breakfast/brunch as "selective occasions."
**Mitigation:**
- Build the P&L so **weekends alone cover fixed costs**. Treat weekday sales as upside.
- A weekday revenue engine that isn't classic breakfast: a pastry/coffee retail counter, a business lunch menu, events (corporate breakfasts, showers).
- Consider **closing 1–2 weekdays** (e.g., Mon/Tue) if traffic doesn't justify staffing.
- Protein-forward, smaller-portion menu items and a strong zero-proof list.

### 2. Soft consumer spending and a local downturn 🔴
**Evidence:** Creative Loafing called H1 2026 "brutal" for Tampa Bay restaurants, citing a "tanking economy, rising inflation," and closures including **Rome + Fig** (a 2026 brunch runner-up), TNT Burger, Gen X Tavern and SoHo Saloon. Local reporting described fears of a slow summer driven by gas prices, a weak snowbird season and inflation at a three-year high. Popmenu: 68% of consumers say they're cutting back, and weekly restaurant spend fell from ~$115 (Jun 2025) to ~$90 (Feb 2026).
**Mitigation:**
- Make value clear at upscale prices: prix-fixe "brunch for the table," carafes, and generous hospitality touches.
- Keep the menu tight to limit food waste. Use dynamic scheduling.
- Hold a **6-month working-capital reserve**, not 3.
- Stress-test against the conservative case (about $2.2M sales) in [05-operating-costs.md](05-operating-costs.md).

### 3. Build-out cost overrun and delays 🔴
**Evidence:** First-time operators overrun budgets by **20–35%** (NRA via VantaInsights). Full-service build-outs take 5–8 months. Inspections drive the schedule. A failed inspection can add 2–4 weeks of carrying costs.
**Mitigation:**
- Prioritize **second-generation space** (30–50% savings). Several 2026 closures created candidates.
- Get a GC with Tampa restaurant experience. Use a guaranteed maximum price (GMP) contract and a 15% contingency.
- Hire a permit expediter. Negotiate a rent-commencement date tied to the CO, not a calendar date.
- Negotiate a landlord tenant-improvement allowance.

### 4. Labor cost escalation and talent scarcity 🟠
**Evidence:** Florida's minimum wage hit **$15.00 on Sept 30, 2026** (tipped $11.98), with inflation indexing from 2027. Tampa BLS medians for hosts, servers and cooks were below $15–$17 as recently as 2023, so the wage floor is resetting the whole scale. Skilled cooks are in demand from MICHELIN-level restaurants and hotels. Florida E-Verify rules (25+ employees) narrow the labor pool.
**Mitigation:**
- Daytime-only hours as a recruiting advantage. A clear career ladder and bonus pools.
- Menu engineering for labor efficiency (batchable components, limited stations).
- Technology: KDS, handheld POS, reservation-driven scheduling.
- Consider a service-charge model to fund BOH pay equitably (requires labor counsel).

### 5. Hurricanes, flooding and insurance 🟠
**Evidence:** Hurricanes **Helene and Milton (2024)** shut many Tampa Bay restaurants. Florida restaurant sales fell 17.1% year over year in Milton's week (Black Box Intelligence). Closures were still being blamed on hurricane recovery costs in 2026 (e.g., Tuttorosso). The Rusty Pelican needed months of Helene repairs. Florida commercial property policies carry separate **named-storm deductibles** (a % of insured value), and premiums are high.
**Mitigation:**
- **Site selection:** check the FEMA flood zone and elevation (much of South Tampa, Bayshore, Davis Islands, Harbour Island and the Channel District is low-lying). Prefer upper-floor or elevated sites, or buildings with flood barriers.
- Get insurance quotes **before** signing the lease. Buy **business interruption** coverage including civil-authority closures and utility outage, and price flood insurance separately.
- A hurricane preparedness plan: shutters/panels, generator hookup or a refrigeration contingency, inventory minimization ahead of storms, and a staff communication tree.
- Keep cash reserves sized for 2–4 weeks of closure each season (June 1–Nov 30).

### 6. Alcohol licensing / zoning 🟠
**Evidence:** The full-liquor **SRX** license requires ≥2,500 sq ft of service area, ≥150 seats, and ≥51% food/non-alc revenue. The alternative is a quota 4COP costing **$210K–$250K** in Hillsborough. City of Tampa AB Special Use 2 approval can require City Council hearings and distance-separation waivers.
**Mitigation:** Make the lease contingent on AB approval. Prefer sites with existing restaurant AB approval. Design to meet SRX thresholds. Monitor the food-to-alcohol ratio monthly, which matters most if offering bottomless drinks. Keep beer/wine (2COP) as a fallback.

### 7. Competition 🟠
**Evidence:** Established, well-funded groups (Ponte, Pierola, Bern's Steak House Group's new Peddler + Potter opening fall 2027 in SoHo, Ciccio, Oxford Exchange) and MICHELIN-recognized venues (The Pearl, Predalina, Psomi). See [03-competitors.md](03-competitors.md).
**Mitigation:** Commit to a sharp, ownable concept (daytime-only, a pastry engine, a Florida identity, event-first design). Avoid "me-too" Benedicts and bottomless-mimosa positioning. Invest in hospitality culture; reviews compound.

### 8. Seasonality 🟡
**Evidence:** Tourism and snowbird peaks run Jan–Apr. Summer brings heat, rain and hurricane season. Restaurant Month in June exists partly to fill the slow period.
**Mitigation:** A summer locals program, events and cooking classes, flexible staffing, and inventory control. Model monthly cash flow, not annual averages.

### 9. Lease and real estate 🟡
**Evidence:** Retail vacancy is very tight (~3.5% Tampa Bay, Q2 2025), which pushes rents up. Waterfront and older sites face redevelopment (the Rusty Pelican site is becoming a 21-story tower). Parking is scarce in SoHo and Hyde Park.
**Mitigation:** Long initial term with renewal options; occupancy target ≤8–10% of sales; relocation/redevelopment clauses; valet or parking agreements; co-tenancy clauses in new developments.

### 10. Liability 🟡
**Evidence:** Florida dram-shop exposure is limited but real, especially when serving minors or known habitual drunkards. Bottomless programs raise over-service risk and pour cost. Food safety risk is concentrated in eggs, hollandaise and raw seafood.
**Mitigation:** Responsible-vendor training (FS 561.705 protections), time-limited or carafe-based drink programs instead of "unlimited," liquor liability insurance, HACCP-style procedures for eggs and sauces, and certified managers on every shift.

### 11. Reputation at opening 🟡
**Evidence:** Social-media-driven discovery cuts both ways. Early Google, Yelp and TripAdvisor ratings anchor visitors' choices.
**Mitigation:** A soft opening at reduced capacity, staged influencer previews, a manager on every weekend table, and 48-hour review responses.

---

## Go / No-Go gates (recommended)

1. **Site gate:** AB zoning path confirmed, flood zone and insurance quote acceptable, occupancy cost ≤10% of conservative-case sales.
2. **Capital gate:** fully funded through opening **plus** a 6-month reserve and a 15% contingency.
3. **Concept gate:** a clear point of difference, validated by guest testing or pop-ups (e.g., pastry pop-ups at markets, a brunch pop-up in a partner venue).
4. **Team gate:** GM and Executive Chef with upscale, high-volume brunch experience signed before construction starts.
